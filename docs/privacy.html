🌦️ Weatherlet

Privacy policy

Last updated 30 October 2026

The short version

Weatherlet has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. We don't collect or sell personal information. Your places and settings stay on your phone.

What is stored on your device

Your saved places, settings (profiles, units, alert and commute times) and the most recent forecasts, kept in the app's private storage. Uninstalling the app deletes all of it.

What is sent over the network

To get a forecast the app sends the coordinates of each saved place to Open-Meteo.com. Text you type when searching for a city is sent to Open-Meteo's geocoding service. For places in the United States, coordinates are also sent to the US National Weather Service to look up official warnings. UK warnings (Met Office), European warnings (MeteoAlarm) and El Niño data (NOAA) come from national or global feeds that are downloaded in full and filtered on your device, so no location is sent for them.

As with any internet request, these services can see your device's IP address. Their own privacy policies apply to what they do with it.

Location

Location is optional. If you choose "Use my current location", the app asks for approximate location permission and reads it only while the app is open, to update the forecast when you travel. To turn your coordinates into a readable city name, your phone's built-in geocoder (usually provided by Google Play services) may be used. You can revoke the permission any time in system settings, or remove the 📍 place in the app to stop following your location.

Optionally, you can turn on "Update my location hourly". Then the app also checks your approximate location about once an hour while it is closed, which requires the "Allow all the time" location permission. This is off by default, only city-level location is used, and it only keeps the forecast matching where you are. Turn it off in Settings at any time.

Notifications

Alerts are created on your device from forecast data. No notification service or push server is used.

Children

Weatherlet is a general-audience app and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes — for example if advertising is added — the updated policy will be shown in the app and the date above will change.
